NotebookLM is especially useful because its answers are grounded in the sources you supply. It can cite those sources so you can inspect the basis of its summaries.

However, there's an important limitation: four AI systems agreeing on something doesn't necessarily make it true. NotebookLM can reveal consensus, but we still need independent evidence for factual claims.

## 2. How I would organize the NotebookLM research

I suggest creating one NotebookLM notebook for our initial research topic.

Notebook title: `Elder Scholars — After the Vibe`

Add the four original AI responses as separate sources, rather than combining them into one document.

That allows us to ask questions such as:

- Where do all four AI systems agree?
    
- What did one AI identify that the others overlooked?
    
- Which responses provide concrete evidence of actual company practices?
    
- What claims contradict one another?
    
- What questions should a software engineer ask before deploying AI-generated software?
    

For the first comparison, I would exclude the follow-up conversations. We want to compare answers to the identical original question before introducing additional context.

## 3. How NotebookLM works with Obsidian

Here's the convenient part: NotebookLM supports Markdown (`.md`) files directly. You don't need to convert your Obsidian notes to PDFs.

Gemini Notebook Help+1

Our workflow will be:

1. Write and preserve the AI responses in Obsidian.
    
2. Upload the four Markdown files to NotebookLM.
    
3. Ask NotebookLM to compare them.
    
4. Copy the resulting analysis into `03-NotebookLM/Q01-Synthesis.md`.
    
5. Commit the new research to GitHub.
    

One caveat: ordinary uploaded Markdown files are snapshots. Editing a note in Obsidian doesn't automatically update the uploaded NotebookLM source. You'll need to replace or re-upload it when necessary.
