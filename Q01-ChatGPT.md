## 1. The central concept

I'd organize the presentation around a question:

"What Happens After the Vibe? From AI-Generated Code to AI-Managed Software Engineering."

The presentation can explore three stages:

1. The Vibe
    
    An engineer describes a problem in natural language. AI generates a working software solution.
    
2. After the Vibe
    
    The software needs testing, debugging, documentation, security review, deployment, and maintenance. How much of that can AI perform?
    
3. The AI-Managed Engineering Process
    
    What happens when AI participates in requirements analysis, architecture, programming, testing, deployment, and ongoing maintenance? What responsibilities remain with the human engineer?
    

That third stage could become the most interesting part of your lecture.

You could even ask a new question during the presentation, collect an AI response, and add it to the repository in real time.

That would demonstrate the iterative process rather than merely describe it.

## 5. The engineering experiment

I would eventually introduce a small software project that the AI systems help develop.

The interesting part isn't whether AI can generate a program. We already know it can.

The interesting questions are whether AI can recognize its own mistakes, test its assumptions, document the program, handle changing requirements, and maintain the result.

For example, we might deliberately change a requirement after the program is working and ask AI to identify every component affected by the change.

That gives the Elder Scholars audience something concrete to observe.

It also provides a natural opportunity to discuss the distinction between generating code and engineering software.

## 6. My suggested starting point

I wouldn't create the entire presentation structure in detail yet. I'd establish the GitHub repository, open it as an Obsidian vault, and create the first research question and response notes.

Then we can work incrementally, allowing the questions, comparisons, and demonstrations to determine how the presentation develops.

Do you envision Obsidian serving as the actual presentation interface, with you navigating notes live in front of the audience, or do you intend to use Obsidian to develop the material and eventually generate a more traditional slide presentation?

Both approaches work, but the choice will influence how we organize the Markdown files from the beginning.
